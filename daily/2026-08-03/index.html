Why this is hard for models too

In the paper, Ye, Cui & Hadfield-Menell (arXiv:2603.12277, ICML 2026) showed LLMs infer "who's speaking" from style — hedge words, first-person planning phrases, scratchpad formatting — rather than role tags. Their CoT Forgery attack, which plants a fake chain-of-thought inside untrusted input, hit a ~60% success rate on the StrongREJECT benchmark and ~61% in agentic data-exfiltration tests, versus near-zero for plain injection attempts.

Their proposed mitigation, destyling — stripping reasoning-style language from untrusted input before it re-enters context — cut that exfiltration success rate to roughly 10%. Meaningful, but not a fix: the researchers are explicit that no amount of training fully closes this gap.

Tells that should raise suspicion, in this game and in real transcripts: reasoning that references authorization the agent was never actually given, reasoning that appears inside quoted tool output or a pasted document rather than the model's own turn, and reasoning that conveniently short-circuits a safety check right when it matters most.
